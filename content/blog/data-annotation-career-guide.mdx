Data annotation — labeling the text, images, and audio that AI models learn from — is often the first paid job people get in AI. It's accessible, usually remote, and doesn't require a degree. But is it a *career*, or just a side gig? The honest answer: it can be either, depending on how you approach it.

## What data annotation involves

Annotators turn raw data into labeled examples a model can learn from. Depending on the project, that might mean:

- Drawing bounding boxes around objects in images
- Tagging text by sentiment, topic, or intent
- Transcribing and timestamping audio
- Ranking or correcting AI-generated responses

Projects come with detailed guidelines, and your job is to apply them consistently — thousands of times.

## What it pays

Pay varies a lot by task type, complexity, and your location:

- **Entry-level annotation:** often \$7–15/hour or an equivalent per-task rate
- **Specialized annotation** (medical, legal, multilingual): meaningfully higher
- **Evaluation and QA roles** (reviewing others' work or AI output): higher still

## The skills that help you earn more

Annotation looks simple, but the top earners share a few habits:

1. **Ruthless consistency.** Models suffer when labels contradict each other. Reliable annotators get more work.
2. **Guideline literacy.** Reading instructions carefully — and asking good questions when they're ambiguous — separates pros from the pack.
3. **Speed *with* accuracy.** Quotas are real, but rushing into errors gets you removed. Build accurate speed through practice.
4. **A niche.** A second language, domain expertise, or comfort with technical content opens higher-paying projects.
