AI training and evaluation work is one of the most accessible ways to earn money in AI today — often remotely, often part-time, and frequently without a traditional computer-science degree. Companies building large language models need thousands of knowledgeable people to rate outputs, write reference answers, label data, and stress-test models. This guide shows you how to land your first role.

## What is an "AI training" job, exactly?

It's an umbrella term for the human work that makes AI models better. Common varieties include:

- **Model evaluation** — rating AI responses for accuracy, helpfulness, and safety
- **Coding evaluation** — reviewing AI-generated code and ranking solutions
- **Data annotation** — labeling images, text, or audio to build training datasets
- **Domain expert review** — using your expertise (law, medicine, finance, a language) to judge AI outputs in that field

The common thread: you bring human judgment that the model is trying to learn from.

## Who's hiring

A handful of marketplaces specialize in connecting this work to contributors. You'll see roles from platforms like **Mercor**, **micro1**, **Turing**, **Handshake**, and others. Rates range widely — from entry-level annotation around \$8–15/hour to expert evaluation roles paying \$50–130/hour or more.

## What makes a strong beginner application

You're competing with other applicants, so small things matter:

1. **Clear, correct written English.** Most of this work is about explaining *why* an answer is good or bad. Clarity is the skill.
2. **A relevant strength.** Fluent in a second language? Strong in math? A working developer? Lead with it — many roles are domain-specific.
3. **Attention to detail.** Annotation and evaluation reward consistency. Mention any experience that shows you follow guidelines precisely.
4. **A sample of your reasoning.** If asked to rate something, don't just pick a score — justify it in a sentence or two.

## A realistic first-week plan

- **Day 1–2:** Pick two or three roles that match your strengths. Read each listing's requirements carefully.
- **Day 3:** Apply on the original platform. Complete any qualification task slowly and carefully — these filter out most applicants.
- **Day 4–7:** While you wait, practice. Take a few AI responses and write one-paragraph evaluations. This is the exact muscle the job uses.

## Avoiding scams and bad listings

Legitimate AI training work **never** asks you to pay a fee to start, and applications always happen on the marketplace's own site. Be cautious of anyone who:

- Asks for payment, crypto, or gift cards up front
- Requests sensitive personal data before you've been selected
- Promises guaranteed income with no qualification process

## Turn your first job into a career

Your first role is a foot in the door. From there:

- Ask for feedback and raise your quality scores — higher-rated contributors get more and better work
- Move from annotation toward evaluation, then toward domain-expert or engineering roles, which pay more
